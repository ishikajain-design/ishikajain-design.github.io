Reflections + learnings

Designing fast, designing for people.

Speed with purpose

Creating social media graphics for Cal Poly STUNT made me much faster and more decisive in Photoshop. Final scores could be released at any moment, and I often had about 30 minutes to turn them into a polished post. I learned how to work quickly without losing the energy, clarity, and brand consistency that made each update feel worth sharing.

Turning vague feedback into design decisions

Working as a graphic designer for ReadyMoms taught me how to listen beyond a client’s exact words. Feedback like “make it pop” or “elevate it” was not always a clear instruction, so I learned to ask what was really missing: stronger hierarchy, more contrast, a clearer message, or a visual direction that better connected with the audience. Iteration became how I translated those instincts into thoughtful choices.

Design as part of marketing

At Presso, I began to understand how visual design helps a brand gain traction on social media. Consistent posting, recognizable content, and adapting assets for different formats all mattered—not just one strong graphic. I also grew more confident in video editing, learning how motion and pacing can make a message more engaging.

These experiences taught me that strong graphic design is not only about making something look good. It is about responding quickly, listening closely, and creating work that people want to notice and engage with.
